### 3.5 `trpl-drip-campaign` — the systems piece

> **Seven Systems, One Visitor: An Automated Lifecycle for a New Institution**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Role | Chief Communications Officer |
| Stack | Constant Contact · ACME · Shopify · Clover · DonorPerfect · postal append · wealth screening |
| Result | 41 emails · 5 journeys · 9-level priority hierarchy |
| Target length | ~1,400 words |

This is the page that proves you're an operator, not just a communicator. It's also the most *portable* study on the site — every institution has this problem.

**Outline**

1. **The brief** — Six source systems (web forms, ACME ticketing, ACME membership, Shopify, Clover, DonorPerfect) each holding a partial view of the same person. A donor who buys a ticket and eats lunch is three contacts. Opening day would generate tens of thousands of these.
2. `case-stats-row` — 41 emails drafted · 5 journeys · 9-level priority hierarchy · 6 source systems unified
3. **One profile, permanent signals** — match on email, then source-system contact ID, with a manual review queue for ambiguity. Source is a permanent tag that never gets overwritten; signals stack. Match success rate is the named data-quality metric.
4. **The priority hierarchy** — the core design decision, and a table on the page. Nine levels, with the counterintuitive one called out: **cumulative giving outranks membership at $1,000+**. A $1,200 donor who is also a member gets the donor journey, not the member journey.
5. **Five journeys** — Soft Lead Nurture (4 emails), Visit Prep (5 before / 6 after), Member (14), Donor (9), plus 3 cross-cutting.
   - **The lead-time gate is the good detail:** 5+ days out gets the full five-touch cadence; 2–4 days skips T-5; 0–1 days merges T+0 with T-24h content and leans on a morning-of SMS. The sequence compresses to fit the buyer instead of firing on a calendar.
   - **Two membership offers, deliberately different:** T+5 tactical ("apply your ticket to a membership") and T+30 narrative ("come for the man, leave with a mission") — aimed at different decision-makers.
6. **Wealth screening without blocking the send** — nightly async batch, by design. Today's tier uses existing capacity tags; tomorrow's uses updated ones. Produces `member_prospect` and `upgrade_candidate` for MGO review.
   - **Pull quote:** *"Without ongoing screening, a high-capacity member sits in standard automation giving $150 a year when they could be giving thousands."*
7. **Guardrails** — max 1 email per 48 hours, max 8 per 30 days, enforced platform-wide rather than per-sequence. Tier 5 ($50K+) suppressed from all automation. Donors $5K+ never get an automated ask below their lifetime average gift. New members protected from upgrade asks for 60 days.
   - **Pull quote:** *"The rule that prevents $100 asks from preventing $10K gifts."*
8. **250 miles** — the distance band splitting local from distant member content, built on the premise that many members will rarely visit. Distant members get digital benefits as the headline.
9. **Configuration, not engineering** — *"The work is configuration, not engineering."* Why Constant Contact over a custom build.
10. **Why it mattered**

**Asset advantage:** the interactive flowchart at the planning URL screenshots beautifully. Master Flow, Visit Prep, and the Donor Tiers table are three strong figures.

### 3.8 `trpl-labs` — the toolkit

> ⚠️ **REVISED Aug 29, 2026.** This section was scoped around five repos. A full org audit found **39 repositories, ~25 of them shippable tools**. See `TRPL-REPO-INVENTORY.md` for the complete inventory. The outline below still holds for the five deep-dive products, but the framing and stats are replaced, and this page should probably become **two** pages — see the note at the end of this section.

> **labs.trlibrary.com: 25 Tools, Zero Servers**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Role | Chief Communications Officer (build & ship) |
| Stack | Vanilla JS · GitHub Pages · GitHub Actions · no backend, anywhere |
| Result | ~25 shipped tools in 5 months · $0 hosting |
| Target length | ~1,700 words |

This is the best *engineering* page you'll have, and it has a genuine thesis rather than being a grab bag.

**The thesis:** every product runs as static files on GitHub Pages under a `*.labs.trlibrary.com` subdomain. Where fresh data is needed, the cost is pushed into a scheduled GitHub Action, not a server. There is no backend anywhere in the in-house portfolio, and no API key ever reaches a browser. Git is the database — every number on screen is also a line in version history you can diff.

**Outline**

1. **The brief** — a new institution with a two-person comms shop and no engineering team. Every tool a vendor would build costs $40K and an annual contract. The constraint became the architecture.
2. `case-stats-row` — ~25 tools · 5 months · 0 servers · $0 hosting
3. **The pattern** — a short section on the shared architecture, so the five that follow read as one system.
4. **Trip Planner** (`trip.labs.trlibrary.com`, Jul 2026) — a 10-step rule-based wizard. Leads with desire, not logistics: "What do you want to see & do?" is step 1; origin is step 6. Heuristic scheduler with real pace budgets (~6/8/10h days), time-anchored events placed only on days they actually run, meals inside real opening hours. Routing intelligence folds stops within ~110 miles into the Medora block as day trips and turns farther ones into en-route legs with gateway overnights. Open-jaw airport recommendation that greys out rental companies that would strand your car. Shareable permalink in the URL hash, `.ics` export with floating local time so events read correctly across the Mountain Time boundary.
   - **The best line in the repo:** *"It's a rule-based wizard — deterministic, no AI, nothing to hallucinate."* In a portfolio full of AI work, choosing *not* to use AI is the more interesting claim.
   - **The data-integrity system deserves its own paragraph:** a weekly Action scrapes five regional sources, and on failure *preserves prior events and opens a GitHub issue* — "one broken scrape never empties the planner." A second Action link-rot-checks every booking URL and diffs advertised show seasons, but never edits data: *"automation watches the hours/seasons and tells us when to look, but a person makes the change."*
   - Stats: 43 road-trip stops, 60+ Medora items, 112/112 items with an image, **0 axe-core violations** (WCAG 2.1 AA).
5. **TRC Widget** (`trc.labs.trlibrary.com`, Jul 2026) — 139,714 archival items across 52 partner institutions, made searchable.
   - **Lead with the problem statement verbatim:** *"The TRC's advanced search exposes seven facets but offers no autocomplete on any of them. The underlying data is a controlled Library of Congress vocabulary… so a visitor typing 'henry cabot lodge' gets zero results while 'lodge' surfaces 'Blodgett'. The vocabulary is excellent; the door into it is the problem."*
   - The fix: cache the taxonomy as static JSON via a weekly Action. Facet searches cost the TRC's WP Engine instance nothing, and their flaky full-text endpoint is never in the critical path.
   - **The graph finding is the page's showpiece:** *"Roosevelt sits on 51% of all edges"* — so a second precomputed layout removes his direct links, and the real communities appear (the Army command around Corbin and MacArthur; the White House staff around Loeb and Cortelyou). *"That's the six degrees, and it only appears once the sun is out of the frame."*
   - **Include the reversal.** *"The first version hand-rolled its force layout to keep the project dependency-free. That was the wrong call — a graph layout is exactly the thing not to hand-roll, and the result looked it."* Case studies that admit a wrong turn read as true.
   - Sizes: 6.9 KB gzipped search widget, 22 KB graph. Ethics section on scraping someone else's API: *"the point is that they never notice."*
6. **Medora Weather** (`weather.labs.trlibrary.com`, Jul 2026) — built in a day.
   - **Thesis line:** *"Only that Action ever calls api.weather.gov — the widget and images are served as static files from GitHub Pages, so no amount of visitor traffic touches the government API."*
   - Dual output — a JS widget *and* eight hotlinkable PNG/SVG variants — which is what lets non-technical staff drop weather into an email, a Drupal block, or a signage screen without a developer.
   - Failure design: NWS unreachable → falls back to last published forecast, flags it stale, shows a "data delayed" note instead of going blank. Active alerts tighten polling from 15 minutes to 5.
   - The honest caveat, kept: *"this is an ambient display, not a life-safety alerting channel."*
7. **Benefactor Kiosk** (`gratitude.labs.trlibrary.com`, Apr–Jul 2026) — donor recognition on a 60" 4K touchscreen. **The v1→v2 story is the whole section**, and it's the most valuable product lesson in the set:
   - v1 computed donor society from dollar thresholds. The real data didn't work that way — *"Each donor carries a pre-assigned BENEFACTOR_SOCIETY value. The kiosk should read that field directly and stop computing tiers from dollars."* Read the field. Stop being clever.
   - v1 built a bespoke 4K on-screen QWERTY keyboard with diacritic normalization. v2 deleted it in favor of a level selector plus an A–Z index. The code was retired, not removed.
   - ~5,466 recognized records across 13 societies, with Founding Member (2,442) and Supporter (2,432) alone accounting for ~4,874 — *which is exactly why the interaction model mattered at this volume*.
   - **No dollar amounts displayed.** Recognition by tier, never by gift size — standard donor-wall practice, and a privacy decision worth stating.
   - Kiosk-specific engineering rarely seen elsewhere: burn-in mitigation, 12+ hours/day unattended, *"any uncaught error returns the kiosk to Attract mode rather than showing an error screen."*
   - Design metaphor: *"Across the night sky, stars connect to create something greater than themselves."*
   - ⚠️ Private repo, donor PII. Screenshots must use synthetic data — the generator exists in-repo.
8. **Rough Rider: The TR Adventure** (`trgame.labs.trlibrary.com`, May–Jun 2026) — the fun one, and a real engineering artifact. TR's whole life 1858–1919 across 10 chapters, each a 2D platformer over an AI-rendered backdrop ending in its own mini-game and a learning recap. 40 fact achievements, 20 historically-motivated enemy types, characters drawn entirely in code with TR visibly aging and changing costume across all ten chapters. **All audio synthesized at runtime with Web Audio — no audio files** — ten chiptune themes and per-enemy defeat sounds.
   - The mobile work is the serious part: touch controls build nothing on desktop and feed the same key sets, and iOS Safari's `100vh`-behind-the-toolbars problem is solved by measuring `window.visualViewport` and letterboxing a 16:9 box inside the actually-visible area.
   - **The marketing hook:** clearing all 10 chapters unlocks a free youth ticket promo code — with the honest note that *"base64 is light obfuscation, NOT security… the real limits must live in your ticketing system."*
   - Sourced from the Library's own `Book_Text` collection, with dates and quotations verified against source texts. Editorial call worth quoting: TR's death is placed in the closing legacy screen rather than inside the Amazon chapter, to keep each chapter period-accurate.
   - ⚠️ Currently robots-blocked (`robots.txt`, commit literally titled "Block search engines"). Confirm the soft-launch posture before linking it publicly from your portfolio.
9. **The rest of the shelf** — a compact grid covering the other ~20 tools rather than prose on each: LinkChecker, PhotoGallery, Reviews, TicketingWidgets, SocialCalendar, DigitalInvite, TRTimelineWidget, FamilyTree, CampusMap, Quizes, AcquiaDAM-Photo-Embed, LBC-Dashboard, NewsletterBuilder, TRAnniversaries, elkhorn-panos. One line and a link each.
10. **What we learned** — the constraint was the feature. No servers meant no procurement, no vendor, no annual contract, and no 3 a.m. page. Scheduled Actions are a legitimate backend for anything that changes daily rather than per-request.
11. **Why it mattered**

#### ⚠️ Recommendation: split this into two pages

~25 tools will not fit in one readable case study. Proposed:

- **`trpl-labs`** — the architecture story. The thesis, the five deep dives (TripPlanner, TRC-Widget, MedoraWeather, BenefactorKiosk, TRRetroGame), and the grid of the rest.
- **`trpl-open-source`** — the *adoption* story, and a genuinely differentiated page: nine tools built for TRPL that any small museum can fork and run. LinkChecker and PhotoGallery are 5/5 adoptable; AcquiaDAM-Sync, elkhorn-panos and AcquiaDAM-Photo-Embed are close behind. This is the page that says you don't just ship, you ship for the sector.
